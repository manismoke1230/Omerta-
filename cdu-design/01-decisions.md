# 01 — Decision register

Each decision is closed. Where a decision rests on data I could not obtain, the related hold point in [07-hold-points.md](07-hold-points.md) says what would change it.

| # | Decision | Why | Rejected |
|---|---|---|---|
| D-01 | **Use case: supervised hardware test or burn-in bay**, cooling a few liquid-cooled servers or trays | Production racks at a hyperscale AI site run about 95–140 kW each, and are served by high-volume qualified CDUs. 10 kW only fits a test bay. | Production rack cooling |
| D-02 | **Design a custom unit to preliminary-design level**, written so the same documents also work as a purchase specification | A commercial lab cooler is still the cheaper route if one meets [02-design-basis.md](02-design-basis.md). This package lets a buyer test that claim against real numbers, and lets a builder proceed if none does. | Stopping at "get quotes" |
| D-03 | **Rated point: 10 kW, PG25 at 30 L/min, 32 °C supply; facility water 27 °C at 24 L/min** | 30 L/min covers two 4U GPU servers at the 12 L/min minimum Supermicro publishes, plus bypass flow. A 5 K approach on 27 °C water holds supply at 32 °C, and on 32 °C water holds 37 °C, under the 40 °C server limit. | Sizing flow from heat alone (14.4 L/min) |
| D-04 | **Coolant: inhibited PG25, one product, matched to the campus fluid** | It is the fluid class the tray and cold-plate sources quote, and OCP has current guidance for it. Matching the campus fluid stops trays carrying contamination between bay and production. | Treated water; PG55 |
| D-05 | **Run warm: supply setpoint 32 °C, adjustable 25–40 °C, floor at room dew point + 3 K** | Central Ohio summer dew points reach the low 20s °C. A 32 °C loop cannot sweat. | Chilled-water operation |
| D-06 | **Pressure available to the server loop: 100 kPa at 30 L/min** | Published CDUs offer 50–115 kPa. 100 kPa covers a manifold, hoses, quick disconnects and cold plates with margin. | Sizing to one cold plate's drop |
| D-07 | **Heat exchanger: copper-brazed 316L plate unit, long-plate class (SWEP B25T × 30 plates or equal)** | See [03-calculations.md](03-calculations.md): the short plate fails on margin. Copper braze is acceptable with inhibited PG25, whose cold plates are copper anyway. | Short-plate B10T; gasketed unit; double-wall |
| D-08 | **Single-wall exchanger** | A leak between circuits shows up as a pressure and level change on the small sealed loop, and both fluids are non-hazardous. | Double-wall, which costs approach and money |
| D-09 | **Pump: one Grundfos CME 1-3 class multistage pump, 316 stainless, EPDM seal, built-in variable-speed drive** | It reaches 15 m at 1.8 m³/h at about 78 % speed with headroom for a dirty filter. Wet-rotor circulators top out near 12 m. | Circulator (too little head); twin pumps |
| D-10 | **No redundant pump; spare pump kept on the shelf, isolation valves both sides** | A supervised test bay can stop. Servers protect themselves on loss of flow, and the trip is hardwired. | N+1 pumping |
| D-11 | **Drive speed capped at 85 %** | Keeps dead-head pressure at 3.4 bar g, 15 % under the relief setting, while still delivering 32 L/min against the full dirty-filter curve. | Uncapped drive |
| D-12 | **Pressure scheme: fill 1.0 bar g, relief 4.0 bar g, hydro test 6.0 bar g, all parts rated ≥ 10 bar except the expansion tank (6 bar, isolated during the test)** | Relief sits below typical cold-plate loop limits and above the worst pump case. | Higher relief setting |
| D-13 | **Sealed loop with an 8 L diaphragm expansion tank, no open reservoir** | Avoids air contact, which oxidises glycol. A pressure drop then signals coolant loss without a level sensor. | Open or vented reservoir |
| D-14 | **Filtration: one full-flow 50 µm stainless cartridge filter with differential-pressure transmitter** | Matches the OCP PG25 example range and avoids stripping inhibitor with fine media. | Side-stream fine filtration |
| D-15 | **Temperature control: two-way modulating valve on the facility return (Belimo CCV class, Cv 4.7), fails in place** | Authority is 0.34 at design flow. Fail-in-place keeps cooling if the signal is lost. | Three-way valve; TCS-side bypass control |
| D-16 | **Separate fail-closed shut-off valve on the facility supply** | A drip tray cannot contain a leak the building keeps feeding. This valve closes on a confirmed leak or loss of power. | Relying on the control valve to isolate |
| D-17 | **Pump control on supply-to-return differential pressure, with a minimum-flow bypass set to about 3 L/min** | Flow then follows however many trays are connected, and the pump is never dead-headed when all are unplugged. | Fixed speed; flow control only |
| D-18 | **Industrial PLC with local touchscreen; Modbus TCP to the site monitoring system; hardwired "server load permit" relay** | The unit must protect hardware with the network down. | Software-only shutdown over the network |
| D-19 | **Flow meters: magnetic-inductive on both circuits (ifm SM7000 class)** | No moving parts, works in glycol, and gives a two-sided heat balance of about ±3 %. | Turbine or paddle meters |
| D-20 | **Connections: 1" sanitary clamp ferrules at the cabinet, hose kit to a bay manifold fitted with the trays' own quick disconnects** | Keeps the cabinet independent of tray type. | Fixing one disconnect standard on the cabinet |
| D-21 | **Packaging: 600 × 800 × 1000 mm cabinet, wet parts low over a full drip tray, electrical panel above behind a drip shield, all routine service from the front** | Keeps water below electrics and allows filter, pump and strainer removal without moving the unit. | Rack-mount 4U format |
| D-22 | **Electrical build to UL 508A with an NRTL field evaluation before first energisation on site** | Hyperscale sites generally require listed equipment. A one-off cannot get that any other way. | Unlisted prototype |
| D-23 | **Facility branch piping insulated with a vapour barrier wherever facility water can run below 25 °C** | Facility water at 18 °C is under the summer dew point. | Bare pipe |
