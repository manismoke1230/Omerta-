# Brookshire Leads Columbus Venues Seating 250

**Brookshire is the best overall venue for a 250-guest Columbus wedding.** It is a purpose-built wedding venue in Delaware, about 30 minutes north of downtown. Four things put it first. Its ballroom seats 300, which leaves real room for a dance floor. The ceremony happens on site. Catering, bar, cake, DJ and centerpieces come in one all-inclusive contract. And it has the strongest award record in the metro: 16 Knot "Best of Weddings" wins, including 2024 through 2026, and Hall of Fame status. **The Ohioan Hotel & Event Center** in Lewis Center is a very close second. It is the better choice if you want guests sleeping on site, and it is one of the few venues that publishes a "300 with a dance floor" figure itself. **The Fives** is the strongest downtown option if budget is not the constraint. The bigger lesson is how many famous venues fail at this size. Strongwater, High Line Car House, Mozart's and the Ivory Room are too small. The Columbus Athenaeum is in receivership. Franklin Park Conservatory, Vue, Jorgensen Farms and the Columbus Museum of Art all list 250 as their exact seated maximum, which leaves no room to dance. Plan on roughly **$55,000–$75,000 all-in**. The venue, food and bar portion alone runs about $20,000–$50,000 depending on the venue. Book 12–18 months out for a Saturday. A note on evidence: no venue publishes a floor plan for 250 guests with dancing. Knot award counts come from search excerpts, because The Knot blocked direct access. Most prices are undated listing figures. Treat this ranking as a shortlist to tour, not a substitute for floor plans and written quotes.

## Brookshire wins on seat cushion, awards and turnkey service

The user set no style, budget or date, so "best" here means the venue most likely to give 250 seated guests a good night with the least risk. That rests on four things: room to spare after the dance floor goes in, an on-site ceremony, a strong and recent reputation, and evidence that the venue is operating normally. Brookshire scores best across all four. Zola lists its ballroom at **up to 300 seated**, and WeddingWire and Here Comes The Guide agree on 300 ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/brookshire-event-venue); [WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/brookshire-delaware/0bc53082a9fc5dec.html)). The venue's own site gives no number, and its inquiry form tops out at "250+" ([Brookshire](https://brookshire.biz/wedding-venue-polaris-lewis-center/)). So the 300 is a listing figure, not one the venue has published itself. The package is the most complete in the market. Zola lists **$12,000 off-peak (January–March) and $14,000 peak (April–December)**, plus a 22% service fee and tax. That covers full catering, cake, centerpieces, a five-hour DJ, about 80 linen colors, and a bar with beer and wine; liquor is an add-on. Outside alcohol is not allowed ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/brookshire-event-venue); [WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/brookshire-delaware/0bc53082a9fc5dec.html)). By the researchers' arithmetic, a 250-guest peak Saturday lands around **$22,000–$35,000+** for the venue, food and bar portion.

The reputation case rests mostly on The Knot. The Knot's listing shows **16 Best of Weddings wins, including 2024, 2025 and 2026**, though that comes from a search excerpt because the page was blocked ([The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/marketplace/brookshire-weddings-and-events-columbus-oh-335277)). Brookshire's own site claims Hall of Fame status ([Brookshire](https://brookshire.biz/wedding-venue-polaris-lewis-center/)), which requires four or more wins ([The Knot Help Center](https://helpcenter.theknot.com/hc/en-us/articles/48485231885204-What-is-The-Knot-Best-of-Weddings-Hall-of-Fame)). The award is based on reviews: a vendor needs a 4.5-star average across seven or more reviews in the qualifying year ([The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/marketplace/brookshire-weddings-and-events-columbus-oh-335277)). That makes the 2026 win the best evidence of recent service quality. It matters because Brookshire's newest visible WeddingWire reviews date from mid-2024. Those reviews average **4.9 from 60** ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/brookshire-delaware/0bc53082a9fc5dec.html)). The case against Brookshire is real. A MapQuest aggregate shows **3.0 from 14 reviews**. A 2022 reviewer called the staff "a major letdown," and a 2024 reviewer said the planner "was not ready" ([MapQuest](https://www.mapquest.com/us/ohio/brookshire-event-venue-22557354)). There is also a rain problem. If a 250-guest ceremony moves indoors, it has to go into the ballroom, because the Veranda seats only 200. That means a room flip.

**The Ohioan Hotel & Event Center is the strongest challenger.** On some measures its evidence is better than Brookshire's. Its own wedding page says the Grand Ballroom **"seats 300 with a dance floor"** ([The Ohioan](https://www.theohioan.com/weddings/our-spaces)), and its event chart lists 432 banquet rounds without one ([The Ohioan](https://www.theohioan.com/meetings-events/event-spaces)). Its **WeddingWire rating of 4.9 from 140 reviews** is the deepest review record of any venue in this research ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/edgewater-at-nhcc-lewis-center/083a369651b9d30c.html)). It also has **193 rooms on site**. A **$14 million renovation finished in fall 2025** added a new outdoor courtyard connected to the ballroom ([Nationwide Realty Investors](https://www.nationwiderealtyinvestors.com/news/nationwide-hotel-conference-center-rebranded-renovated)). It ranks second because it is still Nationwide's corporate training hotel. Its per-person menu prices are unpublished, and no wedding reviews since the renovation turned up. Choose the Ohioan over Brookshire if many guests are traveling, or if the MapQuest complaints worry you.

Ownership is worth knowing. Brookshire's site footer places it in the same group, BTTS Holdings, as **The Estate at New Albany, Vue Columbus, WatersEdge, Darby House and The Ellis**, along with Gourmet Fresh Catering and Fiori Florals ([Brookshire](https://brookshire.biz/wedding-venue-polaris-lewis-center/)). That explains why Brookshire and the Estate show identical $12,000/$14,000 package wording on Zola. It also means those six venues share a catering-and-floral system. If Brookshire's date is taken, the Estate's 350-seat Great Hall offers the same package model with more room. The tradeoff is the weakest reviews in the group: **4.4 from 21, with complaints about add-on charges** ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/the-estate-at-new-albany-new-albany/3b755cda1e876b12.html)).

**The Fives** is the clearest downtown fit. Here Comes The Guide lists its Social Hall at **300 seated** and Cvent says 325 ([HCTG](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-venues/ohio/the-fives); [Cvent](https://www.cvent.com/venues/columbus/special-event-venue/the-fives/venue-eddc4b88-e4fa-4ef1-932c-ca57fa4060fb)). It is the only urban venue with separate ceremony spaces, so no flip is needed: a covered rooftop terrace, an amphitheater with 250 chairs (+$3,750), and the hall itself ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/the-fives); [The Fives](https://www.thefivescolumbus.com/)). A live site with a "Bride, 2026" testimonial confirms it is operating. Pricing sources disagree. Zola lists $20,000 off-peak / $30,000 peak, while HCTG lists a $4,000–8,000 rental plus a $3,000–10,000 food and beverage minimum and an 18% service charge ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/the-fives); [HCTG](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-venues/ohio/the-fives)). The same company, Together & Company, owns High Line Car House and operates The Fives ([Columbus Weddings via Issuu](https://issuu.com/columbusdispatch/docs/columbusweddings_fallwinter2020/20)). Couples who love High Line but have 250 guests can get the same team at a room that fits.

**The Ohio Statehouse** is the historic pick, with a clear constraint. Its official FAQ caps weddings at **275** and charges a **flat $10,000 with no discounts "for any reason."** Food comes from nine approved caterers, and L.A. Catering has been the sole bar provider since July 1, 2025. The day ends hard: "Lights go on at 11pm" ([Ohio Statehouse FAQ](https://www.ohiostatehouse.org/Assets/Files/1003285.pdf); [Ohio Statehouse](https://www.ohiostatehouse.org/events/weddings)). **Brookside Golf & Country Club** wins among clubs. Its Zola FAQ gives "up to 275 guests with a dance floor," one of the few figures anywhere that explicitly counts the dance floor. Its WeddingWire reviews run into 2026 ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/brookside-golf-country-club); [WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/brookside-golf-and-country-club-columbus/01c4ce229cbc8485.html)).

**White Willow Meadows** is the best barn in range. Here Comes The Guide updated its listing on June 4, 2026 and shows **350 seated** ([HCTG](https://www.herecomestheguide.com/wedding-venues/ohio/white-willow-meadows)). It is unusually flexible: bring your own alcohol, poured by venue bartenders, and use preferred or outside caterers. Outside caterers pay a 15–18% fee ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/white-willow-meadows/4296f42f0ec67ada.html)). **The Boat House at Confluence Park** is the value pick. It publishes all-in per-guest packages from $50 (no bar) to $94 (four-hour full bar), at the same price year-round, plus a $1,500 four-hour room fee for a ballroom Zola lists at **350 seated**. Reviews run from February 2025 to July 2026, and it holds a Best of Zola 2026 badge ([Zola](https://zola.com/wedding-vendors/wedding-venues/the-boat-house-at-confluence-park)).

## Budget $55,000–$75,000 all-in; the venue line swings by $30,000

National averages describe weddings less than half this size. **The Knot's 2026 Real Weddings Study** puts the average at **$34,200 for 117 guests**, about $292 per guest. Venue spending averaged **$16,000 for weddings over 100 guests**, and the Ohio venue average was $10,510. Those figures come from search excerpts, because the pages blocked direct fetch ([The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/average-cost-reception-venue); [The Knot](https://www.theknot.com/content/average-wedding-cost)). Zola's 2026 report puts the average wedding at $36,000 ([Zola](https://www.zola.com/expert-advice/the-first-look-report-2026)). One Columbus aggregator estimates **$31,429 for 100–150 guests**, about $251 per guest ([Wedding Planner Central](https://weddingplannercentral.com/wedding-cost/columbus-oh)). Another estimates $46,081–56,321 for 150–200 guests ([wedding.report](https://wedding.report/action/wedding_cost.estimate/budgetid/4C7B9E1F-9594-C872-6CA79A19179DEAD2)). Scaling the per-guest figures to 250 gives about $62,750 at the Columbus rate and $73,000 at the national rate. Fixed costs like photography and attire don't grow with headcount, so **$55,000–$75,000** is a sound planning band. That band is the researchers' arithmetic, not a published figure.

Food is the biggest variable at this size. Columbus catering runs from about **$20 to $121 per guest**: a $65 buffet and a $121 served dinner are both local examples ([The Wedding Dossier](https://theweddingdossier.com/wedding-caterers/columbus-oh)). Metro Cuisine averages $49 for plated or buffet service ([WeddingWire](https://www.weddingwire.com/biz/metro-cuisine-catering-and-special-events-columbus/a01c23e278920d06.html)), and The Knot's 2024 national average was about $80 per plate ([Fox Business](https://www.foxbusiness.com/lifestyle/wedding-season-here-how-much-one-cost-2025)). At 250 guests, food alone costs $12,500–30,000. Bar, an 18–25% service charge and sales tax push it to roughly $20,000–40,000. Tax rates differ by source: 2021 documents cite 7.5%, the current Hilton listing cites 8%, and Swan Lake in Delaware County cites 7%, so confirm the rate for your venue's county. At this headcount, a $10,000–20,000 food and beverage minimum clears itself. **The per-person menu price matters far more than the minimum.**

Date flexibility is the biggest lever. **January–March (sometimes April) is the discount window; November–December is priced as peak or holiday, and some farms discount July–August** ([The Wedding Dossier](https://theweddingdossier.com/blog/best-wedding-venues-columbus-ohio)). Published examples show how much this moves the price. The Exchange's Saturday-evening minimum drops from $20,000 to $10,000 off-peak ([CMPE](https://cameronmitchellpremierevents.com/venues/the-exchange/the-exchange-at-bridge-park-pricing-and-policies/)). Hotel blocks should be reserved 9–12 months ahead for May–October dates, and Ohio State football weekends fill the city's hotels ([Zolana Weddings](https://zolanaweddings.com/blog/hosting-with-intention-the-complete-guide-to-wedding-hotel-blocks-in-columbus-ohio/)). For a 2027 Saturday, outreach should start now.

## Ten questions that expose a venue's real capacity

Few venues in this research publish a seated figure that explicitly includes a dance floor. Among those that reach 250, the list is short: the Ohioan (300), Vitria (300), Brookside (275) and Jorgensen (250, at cap). Everything else needs these questions answered in writing.

- [ ] **Floor plan:** Will you send a 250-guest layout with 25–26 rounds of 10, the dance floor, DJ or band, bars, and cake and gift tables, and what is the true maximum with that layout?
- [ ] **Rain plan:** If it rains, where do 250 people sit for the ceremony? Is there a flip, how long does it take, is there a fee, and who makes the call and when?
- [ ] **Catering:** Is catering exclusive? What is the peak per-person price for 250, what is the service-charge percentage, is that charge taxable, and what is the current sales tax rate?
- [ ] **Bar:** Can we bring our own alcohol? Is the bar priced per person or by consumption? How many bars and bartenders will 250 guests get?
- [ ] **Minimums and dates:** What is the food and beverage minimum versus the rental fee for our date, what counts toward the minimum, and what are the Friday, Sunday and January–March rates?
- [ ] **Timeline:** What is the hard end time and the outdoor amplified-sound cutoff, and what does an extra hour cost?
- [ ] **Guest logistics:** Is there parking for 100–125 cars? What do valet or the garage cost? Is there shuttle access, ADA access (elevators, ramps), heat and air conditioning, and permanent restrooms?
- [ ] **People:** Who is our coordinator, what happens if they leave, and can we read wedding reviews from 2025–2026?
- [ ] **Operator stability:** Who owns and operates the venue? Is any sale, receivership, renovation or construction planned around our date? What happens to our deposit if the venue closes or changes hands?
- [ ] **Blackouts and hotels:** Are there matchday, member-event or farm-season blackouts? Which partner hotels offer blocks, on what attrition terms, and does our date clash with an Ohio State home game?

## Conclusion

In Columbus, seat count decides the shortlist for 250 guests more than budget or style does. The city's signature industrial venues (Strongwater, High Line, North 4th Corridor, The Century) top out between 200 and 240. Four of its most decorated venues (FPC, Vue, CMA, Jorgensen) hit exactly 250 with no room to spare. So the realistic field tilts toward purpose-built suburban venues, clubs and hotels. Brookshire, The Ohioan and Vitria are rare in offering 300 seats with recent awards. The second takeaway is that operator risk now matters as much as price. In the last 18 months the Athenaeum went into receivership, the Ivory Room vanished from its operator's roster, the FPC Palm House closed, the Ohio History Center paused events, and Studio 4980 shut down on couples who had paid in full. Ownership is also concentrated: one group, BTTS Holdings, appears to control six of the venues couples will compare (Brookshire, the Estate, Vue, WatersEdge, Darby House and The Ellis).

The practical upshot: compare BTTS venues on room size, ratings and specific coordinators, not on service model, because their packages, caterer and florist are shared. Make the venue's closure-and-sale clause, and a dated floor plan for 250 with dancing, conditions of signing. Those two documents will separate the true contenders faster than any award badge, especially since the best award data in this market sits behind The Knot's blocked pages and still needs checking on the live listings.
